There is a particular kind of trust that builds between a listener and a station.
It is not the trust of an algorithm that has learned your habits. It is the trust
of a person, sitting in a room, choosing the next song because it is the right one.

## The case for human curation

Streaming services are extraordinary at giving you more of what you already like.
What they are bad at is surprise. The unfamiliar track that becomes a favorite,
the one you did not go looking for.

- A resident builds a narrative across two hours.
- The mix has a shape: a beginning, a middle, a release.
- The silence between songs is part of the performance.

## What this means for the product

If the audience comes for the human, the digital experience has to keep the human
*present*. That is a front-end problem as much as an editorial one: the player has
to feel like a live room, the schedule has to feel like a place, and the archive
has to feel like a collection worth returning to.

> Build the software so the curation is the star, not the chrome around it.
